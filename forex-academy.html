01 — Foundations

Forex basics

Forex, or foreign exchange, is the global market where one currency is exchanged for another. Prices are quoted as pairs because buying one currency means selling another.

Currency pairs

EUR/USD means the euro is the base currency and the U.S. dollar is the quote currency. If EUR/USD rises, one euro buys more dollars than before.

Pips & spreads

A pip is a commonly used unit for measuring small price movements. The spread is the difference between the bid and ask price and is one of the trading costs.

Example: If EUR/USD moves from 1.1000 to 1.1050, that is a 50-pip move under the standard four-decimal convention.
02 — Market analysis

How traders study price

No analysis method guarantees a profitable trade. Traders use different approaches to build hypotheses and manage uncertainty.

🕯️ Candlesticks

Each candle can show open, high, low and close for a chosen timeframe. Candles can help visualize momentum, rejection and consolidation.

📐 Support & resistance

Areas where price has previously reacted can become reference points. They are zones rather than perfectly precise lines.

📈 Trend structure

Higher highs and higher lows can describe an uptrend; lower highs and lower lows can describe a downtrend. Market structure can change.

💧 Liquidity

Liquidity describes the availability of orders to transact. Traders may watch areas around previous highs/lows, but a liquidity concept is not a guarantee of future price behavior.

🧱 BOS / CHOCH / MSS

These are popular market-structure terms. Definitions vary between trading communities, so use one consistent rule set when backtesting.

🕳️ Fair Value Gap

An FVG is commonly described as an imbalance across a three-candle sequence. Traders may watch for price to revisit it, but it does not have to fill.

🏦 Fundamentals

Interest rates, inflation, employment, central-bank decisions and economic growth can influence currencies. Major releases can increase volatility.

⏱️ Sessions

Forex trades across global financial centers. London and New York hours are often closely watched because market activity can increase around major overlaps and releases.

📰 Economic calendar

Before trading, check scheduled high-impact events. Unexpected headlines can also move markets rapidly.

03 — Risk management

Protect the account first

Risk management controls how much you can lose when a trade idea is wrong. It cannot eliminate losses.

1% example

If an account is $1,000 and a trader chooses to risk 1% on one trade, the planned maximum loss is $10 before costs/slippage.

Stop loss

A stop-loss order can be used to define an exit point. In fast markets, execution can differ from the requested level.

Risk-to-reward

A 1:3 target means risking 1 unit to target 3 units. A 1:3 ratio does not mean the trade has a 3× probability of winning.

Important: Leverage magnifies exposure. It can magnify losses as well as gains, and in some circumstances losses can be substantial. Learn the rules and protections that apply in your jurisdiction and with your broker.

05 — A repeatable process

Build a trading plan

Define the market

Choose the instruments and timeframes you will study rather than jumping between everything.

Write your setup

Specify the conditions that must exist before entry, invalidation, target and trade-management rules.

Set risk before entry

Know the amount you can lose before placing the trade. Never increase risk simply because a previous trade lost.

Journal every trade

Record setup, entry, exit, reason, screenshot, emotions and result. Review a meaningful sample rather than judging a strategy from one trade.

Test before going live

Use historical testing and/or a demo account to understand how your rules behave before risking real money.

06 — Psychology

Trading discipline

FOMO

Entering because price is moving quickly can lead to impulsive decisions. If the setup is gone, waiting is a valid action.

Revenge trading

Trying to immediately recover a loss can cause risk to increase. A daily loss limit can create a forced pause.

Overtrading

More trades do not automatically mean more opportunity. Trade only when your predefined conditions are present.

07 — FAQ

Common questions

Can Forex make me rich quickly?

There is no reliable method that guarantees quick wealth from Forex. Losses are possible, and leverage can increase the size of both gains and losses.

Should a beginner use leverage?

Beginners should first understand margin, leverage, liquidation/stop-out rules and the potential size of losses. A demo account can be used to learn platform mechanics.

Is a strategy with 1:3 R:R automatically profitable?

No. Profitability depends on many factors, including win rate, transaction costs, execution and how consistently the rules are followed.

What is the safest way to learn?

Start with education, practice chart reading, write rules, backtest, use a demo account and learn the regulations and risks that apply where you live.
